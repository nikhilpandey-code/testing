LAYA QUESTION DESIGN GUIDE
==========================
How to write questions that Laya answers well. The wording of the question and options
is part of the model input, so it decides accuracy more than almost anything else we
control. Facts here come from laya/common.py, laya/agent.py, laya/presets.py, the README's
"Honest limits" and BENCHMARKS.md (checked 2026-11-18). Advice marked "(advice)" is my
recommendation, not something the repository measured.


1. What the model actually sees
-------------------------------
Each question becomes its own sequence:

  [CLS] choice question: <instructions> [SEP]
        [MASK] label_a: description a [MASK] label_b: description b ... [SEP]
        <state as text or JSON> [SEP]

Consequences:
  - The question and every option are read BEFORE the state, and they share a fixed token
    budget (head_max_len: 192 on the English checkpoint, 256 on multilingual and
    typed-decisions).
  - Each option is cut to 48 tokens. If all options still do not fit, each is cut further
    (to a minimum of 4 tokens). Long descriptions are wasted.
  - Label keys are shown to the model verbatim. A key is not just an ID; it is part of the
    text the model reads.
  - The state gets what is left: about 320 tokens on the English checkpoint, about 768 on
    the others (more with max_len). Text past that is cut.
  - Every question is a separate row in the forward pass. More questions cost more time
    (English checkpoint on a T4: 39.5 ms for 1 question, 158.6 ms for 10).


2. Pick the right question type
-------------------------------
  Use choice when      the answer is exactly one of a few named categories.
                       Example: department = billing / technical / sales / other.
  Use noul when        the answer is yes or no and we want P(yes).
                       Example: "Does the customer ask for money back?"
  Use score when       the answer is a level on an ordered scale and "near misses" matter.
                       Example: frustration = calm / concerned / annoyed / very angry.

Rules of thumb:
  - Several independent yes/no facts: ask several noul questions, not one choice with
    combined options. (The triage preset asks is_urgent, refund_requested and churn_risk
    separately.)
  - Score is the weakest type (SST-5: 0.372), and the multilingual checkpoint rarely picks
    the first level (issue #131). If the levels are really categories, use choice.
  - If a noul question behaves strangely on the English checkpoint (it can follow its
    "false:"/"true:" wording instead of the text, issue #156), rewrite it as a two-option
    choice with neutral keys (see section 6).


3. Writing instructions
-----------------------
  - One decision per question. "Is this urgent and about billing?" is two questions.
  - Name the field you mean when the state is a dict. The shipped presets do this:
      "What does the customer want in `message`?"
      "Does `message` communicate time pressure or a deadline?"
  - Be concrete: "Does the customer ask for money back?" beats "Refund?".
  - Keep it short. The instructions share the head budget with the options; when space is
    tight they are cut first (down to 8 tokens).
  - Language (advice): the repository's multilingual benchmarks use English instructions
    with non-English states. Keep instructions in English unless we measure that another
    language works better.


4. Writing choice options
-------------------------
Format: "criteria": {"label": "short description", ...}, or a plain list of labels.

  - Use meaningful snake_case labels, as the presets do: refund, technical_help,
    billing_question, information, cancellation, other.
  - Never use "true", "false", "yes" or "no" as choice labels. The current checkpoints can
    follow the label word instead of the description (README, Honest limits). Use real names,
    or neutral keys "A", "B" with the meaning in the description.
  - Keep descriptions short and distinctive: a few words that separate this option from the
    others, e.g. "refund": "money returned or a duplicate charge reversed".
  - Make options mutually exclusive, and add a catch-all: "other": "none of the other
    options fits".
  - Keep the count small. Accuracy holds up to roughly 20 options; above that the token
    budget runs out (Banking77, 77 labels: 0.425). The budget per option when space is tight
    is (head_max_len - 16) / number_of_options tokens, with a minimum of 4, and that count
    includes the option's own [MASK] marker:
        options   English (192)   multilingual/typed (256)
        5         35 tokens       48 tokens (the per-option cap)
        10        17              24
        20        8               12
        50        3 (min is 4)    4
        77        2 -> 4 min      3 -> 4 min
    If the options cannot fit within max_len at all, Laya raises
    "question 'x' options exceed head_max_len=..." rather than answering.
  - Above about 20 options, pick one of:
      a. raise the budget: predict(..., head_max_len=512, max_len=1024)
      b. predict_shortlist(..., k=20) with an embedding function
      c. split into a coarse question then a fine one (e.g. area -> exact intent)
  - Order: options are scored in the order given, and at 20 options the answer changes in
    15% (English) to 23% (multilingual) of cases when the order is shuffled. Test it (see
    section 9).


5. Writing score levels
-----------------------
Format: "criteria": ["level 0 text", "level 1 text", ...], lowest first.

  - Order from least to most, as the preset does:
      ["calm and neutral", "concerned but civil", "clearly annoyed",
       "very angry or using strong language"]
  - Use 3 to 5 levels with concrete anchors. "1", "2", "3" with no text gives the model
    nothing to compare against. (advice)
  - Know what you get back: answers[qid]["score"] is the expected level (for example 1.84),
    not a level. decide() returns the most likely level (argmax) instead. Pick one and use
    it consistently.
  - On non-English text the multilingual checkpoint seldom chooses level 0. Validate on our
    data, or ask a noul question for the lowest level ("Is the customer calm?").


6. Writing noul (yes/no) questions
----------------------------------
Format: {"type": "noul", "instructions": "...", optional "criteria", optional "labels"}

  - Phrase the question so that "yes" is the event we act on:
    "Does the user threaten to cancel?" rather than "Is the user happy to stay?".
  - criteria may only use the keys "true" and "false" (anything else is rejected):
      "criteria": {"true": "yes, the review is positive", "false": "no, the review is negative"}
  - labels changes only the wording the model sees, not the meaning:
      "labels": {"true": "A", "false": "B"}
    The answer is always P(true). The README suggests this exact pattern when the English
    checkpoint follows its default labels.
  - Alternative that avoids the issue: a two-option choice with neutral keys:
      {"type": "choice", "instructions": "Is this review positive?",
       "criteria": {"A": "yes, the review is positive", "B": "no, the review is negative"}}
  - Thresholds: decide() treats P(true) >= 0.5 as True. For actions with a cost, pick the
    threshold from data (e.g. only auto-refund above 0.9).


7. Designing the state
----------------------
  - Prefer a dict with meaningful keys: {"subject": ..., "body": ..., "from": ...}. Keys
    become part of the JSON the model reads, and instructions can refer to them.
  - Include only fields that matter for the decision. Everything costs tokens; IDs,
    timestamps and HTML waste the state budget. (advice)
  - Put the important content first. Strings and dicts are truncated from the END.
  - Conversations: pass a list of turns oldest first. Lists are truncated from the START,
    so the newest turn survives.
  - Emails: use laya.email_state(subject, body, sender) to strip quoted history,
    signatures and disclaimers (English, Portuguese, Spanish markers) and cap the body at
    3,000 characters.
  - The [MASK] token is removed from state text automatically; no escaping needed.
  - Long documents: multilingual with max_len=8192 reads up to 8,192 tokens, with accuracy
    strong up to about 4,000 tokens of text and more variable beyond.


8. Many questions on one state
------------------------------
  - Send them in one predict call: the state is tokenised once and all questions run in
    one forward pass.
  - Use stable question IDs. With Router(auto_task_detection=True) an exact match of the
    question IDs to a typed-decisions workflow switches checkpoints (e.g. customer service
    = action, category, churn_risk, needs_human, urgency). Avoid those exact ID sets unless
    that is what we want.
  - Schema mode (decide) is a convenient alternative: enum -> choice, boolean -> noul,
    integer with minimum and maximum -> score. Limits: 32 fields, 32 enum values, 10 levels.
    Put the question text in each field's "description".


9. Test a question before relying on it
---------------------------------------
For each new question, on 50 to 100 labelled examples (advice):
  1. Accuracy and answer_confidence per example (not the `confidence` field, which is not
     calibrated for choice and score).
  2. Order test: shuffle the options several times; count how often the answer changes.
  3. Label test: rename the keys (e.g. "billing" -> "A") and compare; a big change means
     the model is reading the label, not the description.
  4. Paraphrase test: two wordings of the instructions; answers should agree.
  5. Language test: if traffic is multilingual, include each main language.
  6. Length test: check that long states still contain the deciding text after truncation.
The repository has tools for 2 and 3 on its benchmark data (research/eval/metamorphic.py:
option permutation and label variants; research/eval/presentation_checks.py: checks for
score-slot bias). They can serve as references for our own checks.


10. Before and after examples
-----------------------------
Weak:
  {"type": "choice", "instructions": "Classify",
   "criteria": {"yes": "", "no": "", "maybe": ""}}
  Problems: vague instruction, boolean-word labels, no descriptions, no catch-all.

Better:
  {"type": "noul", "instructions": "Does `body` ask for money back?"}

Weak:
  {"type": "choice", "instructions": "Which of our 80 product intents is this?",
   "criteria": {... 80 labels ...}}
  Problem: about 3 tokens per option; options become indistinguishable.

Better:
  {"area":   {"type": "choice", "instructions": "Which area is `body` about?",
              "criteria": {"payments": "...", "account": "...", "orders": "...", "other": "..."}}}
  then a second call with only that area's intents (or predict_shortlist with k=20).

Weak:
  {"type": "score", "instructions": "Urgency 1-5", "criteria": ["1", "2", "3", "4", "5"]}

Better:
  {"type": "score", "instructions": "How urgent is the request in `body`?",
   "criteria": ["no time pressure", "within a few days", "today", "blocking work right now"]}


11. Checklist
-------------
  [ ] One decision per question; instruction names the state field.
  [ ] Right type (choice / noul / score); score only for truly ordered levels.
  [ ] Choice labels are meaningful, not true/false/yes/no; short descriptions; catch-all.
  [ ] 20 options or fewer, or a plan (budget, shortlist, two-step).
  [ ] noul phrased so "yes" is the action; criteria keys only true/false.
  [ ] State has only useful fields, important content first; emails cleaned.
  [ ] Gating uses answer_confidence, with thresholds from our data.
  [ ] Order, label and paraphrase tests run on labelled examples.
  [ ] The final wording is frozen before fine-tuning (the model learns that exact wording).
